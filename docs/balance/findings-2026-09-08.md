# Balance findings, 8 September 2026

Source: `pnpm balance --seeds 30` from `src/web`, plus a 100-seed run for the
two melee classes. The generated table is in `lost-idol-2026-09-08.md`. This
note is the interpretation; regenerate the table before trusting the numbers
after any engine or content change.

## Method

The harness drives the bundled Lost Idol quest headlessly through the real
`Adventure` and `Game` classes with a competent-player policy that knows the
map. It collects vases first, takes chests, wakes and fights monsters until the
exit opens, drinks a potion when strength drops below half or when the next
expected hit would be fatal, tops up before starting a fight below 60%, casts
Superzap whenever a charge is available, Healing when potions run out, and goes
straight for the Idol on the final level. Each class is one archetype hero built
through the real Creator rules with 150 gold, a class-appropriate kit, four
potions and three salves.

Because the policy knows the layout and never wastes light, results are
optimistic relative to a first-time player. Because it uses only four potions,
they are pessimistic relative to a player who spends all leftover gold on
restoratives, which the shop allows.

## What drives this

- **Melee is restorative-bound.** A tier-one fight costs a fighter about 1.5
  strength and a tier-two fight about 2, from hits, attack cost, and decay. The
  1% strength decay per action also costs roughly a quarter of current strength
  per thirty squares walked. Passive recovery (vitality / 1100 per turn) is
  negligible, so potions are the only real healing. With the two vases per
  level, fighters enter level two with about eight potions of budget and level
  two's two tier-two monsters exhaust it.
- **Salves are dead weight.** Vitality only falls by hit / 101 per monster
  blow, so the salve half of every restorative is almost never useful. Every
  vase still grants one salve and one potion.
- **Superzap trivialises combat for anyone with the Necronomicon.** Charges
  are Necronomicon value times aura: 12 for the Wanderer archetype, 20 for the
  Cleric, 32 for the Mage, against 16 monsters in the whole quest. Each monster
  then costs exactly one free hit on the reveal or bump turn. The Mage survives
  on 3 strength because it never takes a second hit.
- **Level 3 kills nobody.** The policy goes straight to the Idol, so the final
  level is a walk. Level 2 is the quest's real gate.
- **Gear breakage after the M9 luck save** still costs fighters about two items
  per run at luck 5, usually the main weapon first. Luck 5 saves only half of
  the rolls; the fixture Warrior at luck 6 fares a little better.

All of these follow the BASIC formulas; none is a bug. They are the original's
balance, made visible.

## Candidate adjustments, for decision

None is implemented. Listed in order of how little they disturb canon.

1. Make salves matter: let a salve restore vitality and also part of strength,
   or let the restorative key drink a potion when vitality is already full.
   This alone would roughly double the melee healing budget.
2. Raise passive recovery so that resting on a safe square is a real tactic,
   which the Book of Lore implies it should be.
3. Cap Superzap charges, or give it a per-cast aura cost as Powersurge and
   Healing already have, so magic classes must ration.
4. Leave everything and treat the melee classes as the hard mode, documented
   in the Lore.
