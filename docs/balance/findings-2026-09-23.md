# Fog-harness findings, 23 September 2026 (M13)

Table: `lost-idol-2026-09-23.md`, 50 seeds per class, bot style, and vision.
Regression: `src/web/src/tests/balanceBands.test.ts` now also asserts that a
careful player clears level one without the map and that no fog bot is ever
stranded.

## What the fog bot is

The fog bot is the M12 bot pointed at the engine's `Snapshot`, the structure
the Canvas draws. It knows a square only once the player could see it, and a
monster only once its square is lit. It explores by heading for the nearest
dark square, preferring to keep walking straight on; it treats dark squares on
the map border as walls, the prior a player forms at the first bump; and it
spends its light when most of the squares around it are dark, rationing the
torch across the levels ahead. Everything else, including combat, potions, and
spells, is shared with the omniscient bot, which is unchanged except for one
floor noted below.

The two bots bracket a real player. The omniscient bot is an upper bound on
survival, since no one can do better than knowing the map. The fog bot is a
lower bound for a first visit.

## The bracket (careful style, win %)

| Class | With the map | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbarian | 100 | 0 |
| Warrior | 100 | 0 |
| Wanderer | 100 | 0 |
| Cleric | 80 | 0 |
| Mage | 90 | 2 |

Without the map, no careful bot of any class dies on level one, so level one
remains the warm-up it was meant to be. The deaths come on level three, where
bots arrive with their potions spent.

## Why, and whether it is the bot

Traced before being believed. On level one the careful fog Barbarian stepped
on 130 of 151 floor squares with a revisit ratio of 1.5. That is efficient
systematic exploration, not wandering. It has to be that thorough: the exit
opens only after about four fights, and in the dark a monster is found only by
walking into it or lighting the square beside it.

Every keypress, turns included, costs 1% of strength. A level that takes about
270 keypresses in the dark therefore costs about four potions of pure attrition,
against roughly nine potions for the whole quest: four bought and five from
vases. Careful bots run dry on level two and die on level three.

The pre-registered prediction was that the gap would be widest for the
fighters, through surprise bumps at low strength. The gap is indeed widest for
them, but everyone collapses, and the mechanism is attrition rather than
ambush. The prediction was right in direction and wrong in cause.

## The consequential finding: resting was lost in translation

The archival game ran in real time. Its main loop never waits for a key: line
260 restores vitality / 1100 of strength on every pass, while line 250's 1%
decay needs a keypress. Pausing to think restored strength, which is what the
Book of Lore means by "standing still restores strength slowly." The modern
engine resolves one turn per action, so recovery happens once per keypress and
the Wait key restores almost nothing. Resting disappeared as a tactic, and the
harness shows it is the single biggest balance difference between the modern
game and the original. How large the original effect was depends on the C64
loop rate and the player's tempo, which have not been measured. VICE is
installed and could measure it.

## Other findings

- **Walls stayed dark after a bump.** BASIC line 210 draws a bumped wall; the
  engine did not. Fixed in this tranche, with a test and a browser check. A
  player now sees the wall they walked into.
- **The Idol Vault's exit lies straight in line with its door.** A player who
  kills the guard and walks on into the dark steps onto the exit before seeing
  the Idol, and the quest ends as abandoned without warning. Of the reckless
  fighters that survived to the vault, 95 of 100 abandoned this way.
- **The torch is the purchase that matters most in the dark.** Without it,
  level-two survival falls by 15 to 47 points for every class except the Mage,
  whose spells already deal with what the light would find.
- **Reckless play in the dark is deadly for the Mage.** It explores at low
  strength, and with an armour value of one, a single surprise blow from a
  tier-two monster kills it. This is the style behaving as defined.
- **Completionist and careful rows are identical in the dark**, because both
  die on level three before the completionist rule can matter.

## Harness changes

- `BotView` interface with `omniscientView` and `fogView` adapters; the refactor
  was verified to reproduce the M12 omniscient results exactly across 600 runs
  before any fog logic was added.
- Frontier exploration, the border prior, and the rationed light policy.
- An exhaustion floor: no bot drinks later than strength 1.5, since death comes
  below 1. Without it the reckless bot walked itself to death in the dark. It
  changed two omniscient reckless cells by a few points and nothing else.
- `--vision omniscient,fog` flag; Abandoned, Stuck, Light, and Bumps columns.

## Decisions for Barry

2. Whether the final level's exit should hold the player back without the Idol,
